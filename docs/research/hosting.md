## Legal / data-location constraint

The brief describes Ley 25.326 art. 12 as a data-residency rule. More precisely, AAIP describes it as generally prohibiting international transfers to countries or international organizations that do not provide adequate protection; it is **not a blanket Argentina-only storage mandate**. AAIP lists the EU/EEA and the UK as adequate. **Brazil and the United States are not on the current list.** The AAIP page also describes statutory exceptions and approved model contractual clauses; counsel should confirm which safeguard covers each actual controller, processor, support flow, and subprocessor before processing sensitive records. (AAIP, [International transfers](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/transferencias-internacionales), read 2026-10-20; [Law 25.326, Article 12](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/ley-25326-64790/actualizacion).)

Data-center country and provider’s legal entity are separate checks. A US company storing a bucket in Europe is not equivalent to a European provider; the US provider may still handle account/support/metadata, and a transfer safeguard may be needed. Verify the signed entity, DPA/model clauses, subprocessors, region pinning, and telemetry destinations in the actual contract. The recommendation keeps VPS and backup storage in EU adequate countries with EU entities; that is the simpler route, not a substitute for a legal review of the actual processing arrangement.

### Reliability and single-host recovery

For a single VPS, **Hetzner is the best verified reliability-per-dollar fit**, not because it has no outages, but because it combines low current 8 GB pricing, EU hosting and contracting, transparent IPv4/backup pricing, hourly/monthly billing, snapshots/volumes, and an SLA. The recovery story is still “restore/rebuild, then validate,” not automatic failover. Hostinger is a credible runner-up because it matches the developer’s existing operational experience and includes weekly backups, but its long-term renewal is $14.99/month before extras. OVH is a reasonable alternative when included daily backup and anti-DDoS outweigh its higher baseline; netcup’s weaker/unclear VPS SLA and non-24/7 support are tradeoffs. Contabo’s low advertised price is not decision-grade until the checkout renewal/term is confirmed. DonWeb is the only local candidate, but its unselectable Argentina/US geolocation makes residency ambiguous.

### Operational signals (2024–2026)

Use provider status pages and incident postmortems as evidence, not anecdotes as uptime statistics. Hetzner’s public history includes a 2025 Cloud incident. Hostinger published a 2025 unauthorized-activity incident affecting one server and provides a public status page. netcup has a public status history with panel/network events. OVH, Contabo, and DonWeb also publish status or service notices; this research did not establish a consistent, comparable incident dataset across all vendors. Reports of account suspension or support failures on forums are **unverified customer allegations**, not a reliable estimate of probability. Do not select or reject a provider from isolated posts; instead keep infrastructure-as-code/deployment artifacts, off-site backups, documented account escalation, and a migration plan.

## Sizing: 4 GB vs 8 GB, OSRM and VROOM

**Start production and staging on 8 GB; do not treat 4 GB as established-safe.** The workload is small in user count and daily transfers, but it puts PostgreSQL, Node API, two Compose stacks, OSRM, VROOM, OS processes/page cache, and staging in the same memory budget. Staging also competes for CPU/RAM exactly when testing or deployment causes a spike. Eight GB is a conservative starting point, not a measured minimum; enforce container limits and measure resident memory, swap, OOM events, CPU, disk I/O, and route latency under a representative batch before opening production.

The brief’s “~413 MB” is the size of Geofabrik’s **Argentina country extract**, not a published Tucumán-only clipped extract. First create the polygon extract and record its actual PBF size; then run the exact car-profile preprocessing pipeline and capture peak RSS and resulting `.osrm.*` file sizes. Published OSRM guidance gives rough global-planet examples and explains memory-mapped serving, but does not establish the memory requirement for this regional extract. Planet-scale estimates must not be linearly treated as a proven Tucumán requirement. OSRM supports `--mmap`; that can use file-backed mappings, but does not eliminate RAM/page-cache or disk requirements. Sources: [Geofabrik Argentina extract](https://download.geofabrik.de/south-america/argentina.html), [OSRM tools](https://project-osrm.org/docs/v26.4.0/tools), [OSRM memory discussion](https://github-wiki-see.page/m/Project-OSRM/osrm-backend/wiki/Disk-and-Memory-Requirements) (read 2026-10-20; wiki is community-maintained and older than the current docs).

VROOM’s official material describes routing capabilities and exposes a default thread setting, but provides no dependable memory-per-vehicle or CPU/RAM sizing table for this workload. “~10 vehicles / 100 transfers” sounds modest, but is an inference only: measure the actual matrix size, constraints, response time, and peak RSS using real-shaped, de-identified fixtures. Do not upload production addresses or passenger details to a public benchmark.

**Build OSRM data away from the production VPS** on a temporary runner/host with measured headroom, then publish checksummed, versioned artifacts and deploy atomically. Geofabrik extracts change; preprocessing competes with live service and can exceed small VPS memory/disk. CI is suitable only after a representative build proves the runner’s RAM/time limits and cost; GitHub-hosted runner capacity was not verified here. If preprocessing on the VPS is the only available option, do it off-peak, retain the previous artifact, monitor disk, and test rollback first.

### Monitoring and error tracking pick

- **UptimeRobot free tier** for an external HTTPS `/health` check every five minutes, with an alert channel that is not the VPS. The free plan advertises 50 monitors and is usable for commercial monitoring; configure a health endpoint with no passenger data. This is a vendor-outside check and survives VPS failure. [$0/month](https://help.uptimerobot.com/en/articles/11604710-who-should-use-uptimerobot-s-free-plan) (read 2026-10-20). Do not mistake a green check for database/route correctness; add a separate authenticated synthetic smoke check without sensitive payloads.
- **Self-host GlitchTip**, bound to a private admin path and with SDK scrubbing, is the recommended low-cost error tracker. Budget **$0 incremental** if resources fit the 8 GB VPS, but it shares the same failure domain and does not alert when that host is down. Configure before enabling: strip passenger names, addresses, disability/health flags, request bodies, query strings, cookies, auth headers, and tokens; do not send driver location. GlitchTip’s installation docs describe modest minimum memory, but actual self-hosted resource use and event retention must be measured. [Install](https://glitchtip.com/documentation/install), [pricing/hosted options](https://glitchtip.com/pricing/) (read 2026-10-20).
- **Do not default to sending raw events to Sentry**, even in its Germany region. Sentry advertises EU event storage, but its international data-transfer terms and restrictions on special-category data, plus the possibility that SDK events contain sensitive payloads, require legal/privacy review and aggressive scrubbing first. If the team chooses SaaS, verify current free-tier quota/retention and DPA at signup; those exact limits were not verified in this research. ## Risks and decision gates before production

1. **Privacy / legal:** confirm controller/processor roles, lawful basis/consent for sensitive data about minors, AAIP model-clause applicability, breach procedure, and every backup/support/telemetry transfer. Region selection alone is not enough.
2. **Single-host outage:** host, account, region, storage, and deployment are shared failure domains. Keep off-site encrypted backups under separate credentials; document DNS and rebuild steps; conduct and time a full restore before launch and quarterly thereafter.
3. **Resource pressure:** staging shares production’s memory/CPU/disk. Set cgroup limits and deploy windows; capture OSRM/VROOM/Postgres peak usage and latency from a representative route workload; resize if swap or OOM appears.
4. **Backup correctness:** define RPO/RTO with the business. Hourly logical dumps imply up to roughly one hour of data loss; WAL archiving can improve RPO only if archives, retention, keys, and restore chain are continuously verified. Do not claim point-in-time recovery until it has been restored successfully.
5. **Provider/account risk:** retain image build and infrastructure configuration outside the provider; enable MFA, billing contacts, recovery codes, and an escalation contact. Snapshot-only recovery does not cover suspension, compromised credentials, or provider-wide outage.
6. **Status / SLA interpretation:** SLA service credits do not compensate for lost operations, missed transport coordination, or a missed morning dispatch. Maintain a phone/paper fallback for 6:30 operations.
